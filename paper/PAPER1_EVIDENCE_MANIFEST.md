## Primary ablation evidence

The declared grid contains 238 deterministic range--velocity states. Of these, 132 are physically supportable by at least one declared action and 106 are outside the support of the complete action set. The primary comparison conditions on the 132 supportable states: the gated selector is physically valid in 132/132, while the ungated selector is valid in 18/132 and invalid despite a valid alternative in 114/132 (86.36%). The full-grid ungated invalid count 220/238 (92.44%) is secondary and decomposes into 114 recoverable selection errors plus 106 globally unsupported states.

The gated and ungated selectors use the same 54-action set, nominal QoS screen, and resource ranking; the intended controlled difference is whether canonical physical-feasibility filtering occurs before ranking. The boundary-aware grid is a capability stress grid, not an empirical distribution of real driving states.

## Novelty evidence boundary

The bibliography acknowledges established PC-FMCW waveform/receiver work and recent radar-centric FMCW ISAC parameter reconfiguration. Paper 1 does not claim that adaptive FMCW, PC-FMCW, range/velocity limits, or the standard capability equations are new. Its narrower contribution is the explicit state-dependent hard admissibility layer before finite-action PC-FMCW ranking plus the controlled gate-removal ablation that isolates recoverable unsupported selections.

## Claim boundary

No item in this manifest is a hardware measurement. The high-mobility profile is not a commercial preset. Pilot evidence is simulation. The 86.36% and 92.44% stress-grid values are not field-prevalence estimates. Paper 2 Wilson qualification, B4 evidence, and reliability--availability--computation claims are not used as Paper 1 evidence.

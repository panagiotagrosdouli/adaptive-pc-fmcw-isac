\begin{abstract}
This work studies adaptive phase-coded frequency-modulated continuous-wave (PC-FMCW) integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) for high-mobility vehicular links under Doppler, residual synchronization error, interference, phase noise, and imperfect PHY-state knowledge. Unlike fixed-configuration PC-FMCW feasibility studies, the proposed controller first applies a physical feasibility gate derived from waveform sampling, range, and unambiguous-velocity limits, and then performs reliability-constrained joint PHY adaptation over profile, phase-code budget, transmit-power backoff, and repetition resources. The objective is not to maximize nominal performance, but to identify the subset of operating states in which communication and sensing QoS can be jointly supported with high reliability. In a frozen 1000-seed paired benchmark comprising 72,000 receiver-level evaluations, the robust policy selects 12.23\% of cases and achieves joint QoS in all 1,468 selected cases, corresponding to a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower reliability bound of 99.816\%. This high conditional reliability is obtained at the cost of 87.77\% abstention, and the robust policy does not outperform deterministic or fixed policies in unconditional joint QoS. Supplemental uncertainty and mismatch studies show that the robust controller preserves substantially higher conditional reliability than deterministic joint adaptation under moderate state uncertainty, CFO, Doppler, and interference mismatch, while severe SNR and interference mismatch still produce failure. Prototype runtime rises from 30.71~ms at 64 uncertainty draws to 240.44~ms at 512 draws, so no real-time claim is made for the unoptimized implementation. The results establish an availability--reliability--complexity trade-off and motivate feasible-operating-region characterization as a central design objective for adaptive high-mobility PC-FMCW ISAC.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper extends PC-FMCW ISAC from fixed-configuration functional feasibility toward physics-aware and reliability-constrained operation under high-mobility uncertainty. The proposed methodology separates two questions that are often conflated in adaptive ISAC: whether a candidate waveform/profile is physically capable of supporting the requested range and radial velocity, and whether the remaining admissible candidates satisfy joint communication and sensing reliability under uncertain channel and synchronization conditions. The resulting physical gate prevents impossible profiles from entering the statistical optimization, while the robust controller explicitly trades operating-region availability for higher conditional reliability.

The frozen paired benchmark does not show unconditional superiority of the robust controller. Instead, B4 selects only 12.23\% of evaluated cases, but succeeds in all 1,468 selected cases, with a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower bound of 99.816\%. The deterministic joint baseline selects more frequently but has substantially lower conditional reliability. The supplemental uncertainty sweep strengthens this interpretation: as uncertainty increases, B3 continues selecting a broader region while its conditional joint QoS degrades, whereas B4 progressively contracts its operating region and preserves much higher reliability until the most severe uncertainty setting, where the confidence-qualified 95\% target is no longer met.

Ablation and model-mismatch experiments further show that physical feasibility, uncertainty modeling, and the joint constraint are all consequential. Removing state uncertainty or the joint constraint increases availability but reduces conditional reliability, while admitting physically infeasible actions can invalidate the controller altogether in the evaluated bank. Moderate CFO, Doppler, and interference mismatch favor the robust controller, but severe SNR and interference mismatch remain failure modes. These negative results define the practical boundary of the proposed uncertainty model and are retained as part of the contribution rather than tuned away.

The present implementation also exposes a computational limitation. The 512-draw robust controller requires approximately 240~ms median decision time in the unoptimized Python/GitHub-Actions environment, compared with approximately 0.44~ms for deterministic joint adaptation. Future work should therefore focus on vectorized and parallel uncertainty evaluation, candidate pruning after the physical gate, sequential confidence tests, calibrated surrogate reliability models, and embedded/hardware validation. A hardware implementation is also required before making deployment-level latency, energy, or measurement claims.

Overall, the central result is not that robust adaptation wins every operating point. Rather, physics-gated robust PC-FMCW adaptation makes the reliability boundary explicit: it identifies where joint sensing--communication service can be offered confidently, where the controller should abstain, and where the underlying waveform physics makes the requested service infeasible. This operating-region perspective provides a defensible basis for high-mobility adaptive ISAC design under uncertainty.

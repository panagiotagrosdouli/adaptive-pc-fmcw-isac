\begin{abstract}
This work studies adaptive phase-coded frequency-modulated continuous-wave (PC-FMCW) integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) for high-mobility vehicular links under Doppler, residual synchronization error, interference, phase noise, and imperfect PHY-state knowledge. Unlike fixed-configuration PC-FMCW feasibility studies, the proposed controller first applies a physical feasibility gate derived from waveform sampling, range, and unambiguous-velocity limits, and then performs reliability-constrained joint PHY adaptation over profile, phase-code budget, transmit-power backoff, and repetition resources. The objective is not to maximize nominal performance, but to identify the subset of operating states in which communication and sensing QoS can be jointly supported with high reliability. In a frozen 1000-seed paired benchmark comprising 72,000 receiver-level evaluations, the robust policy selects 12.23\% of cases and achieves joint QoS in all 1,468 selected cases, corresponding to a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower reliability bound of 99.816\%. This high conditional reliability is obtained at the cost of 87.77\% abstention, and the robust policy does not outperform deterministic or fixed policies in unconditional joint QoS. Completed supplemental uncertainty, distribution-shift, mismatch, and calibration studies preserve this reliability--availability interpretation while exposing explicit failure boundaries: on 1,200 paired supplemental units, the B4--B3 unconditional joint-QoS difference is $-0.03667$ with a 95\% paired-bootstrap interval of $[-0.04833,-0.02583]$. Finite-draw calibration shows 0.0833\% decision disagreement at 256 robust draws relative to the high-draw reference in the tested bank. Prototype B4 median runtime is 138.89~ms at 256 draws and 272.81~ms at 512 draws on GitHub Actions infrastructure, so no real-time claim is made for the unoptimized implementation. The results establish an availability--reliability--complexity trade-off and motivate feasible-operating-region characterization as a central design objective for adaptive high-mobility PC-FMCW ISAC.
\end{abstract}

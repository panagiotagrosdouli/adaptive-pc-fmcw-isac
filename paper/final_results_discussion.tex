\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results-discussion}

\subsection{Robustness changes coverage, not just success rate}

The frozen benchmark compares all six policies on the same 12,000 operating-state/seed pairs per policy, for 72,000 receiver-level evaluations overall. The first observation is that the policies do not merely differ in how often a selected action succeeds; they also differ sharply in how often they are willing to act. B0 selects in 92.36\% of cases and attains 59.04\% unconditional joint QoS. B3, which enforces the communication and sensing constraints jointly but treats the estimated state as exact, selects in 19.07\% of cases and attains 16.18\% unconditional joint QoS. B4 is more conservative still, selecting only 12.23\% of cases and abstaining in the remaining 87.77\%.

This loss of coverage is statistically clear. The paired B4--B3 difference in unconditional joint QoS is $-0.03942$, with a 95\% paired-bootstrap interval of $[-0.04292,-0.03600]$ over 10,000 resamples. Relative to B0, the corresponding difference is $-0.46808$ with a 95\% interval of $[-0.47700,-0.45917]$. Robust qualification therefore does not maximize unconditional joint service in this experiment.

The picture is different after conditioning on the states in which a policy chooses to act. B3 attains 84.83\% conditional joint QoS. B4 succeeds in all 1,468 selected cases, for a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower bound of 99.816\%. The robust controller is thus better understood as shrinking the operating region until the retained states are strongly supported by the declared uncertainty model. The hindsight Oracle, which uses true receiver-level state information, selects 66.67\% of cases with unit conditional joint QoS; it is included only as a non-deployable reference.

\subsection{State uncertainty contracts the reliable region}

The uncertainty sweep makes this interpretation more explicit. When the uncertainty scale is zero, B3 and B4 coincide: both select 16.67\% of cases and achieve 100\% conditional joint QoS. As uncertainty grows, their behavior separates. At scale one, B3 selects 18.50\% of states with 87.39\% conditional joint QoS, whereas B4 selects 12.50\% and retains 100\%. At scale two, B3 expands further to 20.33\% selection while its conditional reliability falls to 68.03\%; B4 contracts to 9.75\% and again records 100\% observed conditional joint QoS.

The most severe tested setting shows the limit of this mechanism. At uncertainty scale three, B3 reaches only 62.04\% conditional joint QoS. B4 selects 5.92\% of cases and achieves a 98.59\% observed success fraction, but the one-sided 95\% Wilson lower bound falls to 93.93\%, below the 95\% target. Robust adaptation therefore does not preserve reliability indefinitely. Instead, increasing uncertainty progressively removes states from the confidence-qualified operating region until even the remaining sample support is insufficient.

\subsection{The physics gate prevents a qualitatively different failure}

The ablation study separates uncertainty effects from hard waveform feasibility. FULL\_B4 selects 12.50\% of the ablation bank, achieves 100\% observed conditional joint QoS, and has a 95\% Wilson lower bound of 98.23\%. If state uncertainty is removed, selection rises to 20.00\%, but conditional joint QoS falls to 80.83\%. If the joint sensing--communication constraint is removed, selection expands to 67.25\% and conditional joint QoS falls to 85.50\%. These two ablations describe the familiar reliability--coverage trade-off.

Removing the physical feasibility gate produces a different effect. In the frozen supplemental design, the ungated variant selects 31.67\% of states but achieves 0\% joint QoS. The point is not that every possible ungated controller must fail. Rather, this experiment shows that the optimization becomes ill-posed for the present action set when configurations that violate hard range or unambiguous-velocity limits are allowed to compete with physically admissible actions.

The two waveform profiles make the source of this failure concrete. The short-range profile supports approximately 22.36~m positive-IF range and 8.41~m/s unambiguous radial velocity, while the high-mobility profile supports approximately 56.21~m and 48.67~m/s. The range--velocity plane therefore contains regions in which neither profile is admissible, regions in which only the high-mobility profile is admissible, and an overlap region in which both can be considered. Controller abstention occurs after this physical partition and should not be confused with hard infeasibility.

\subsection{Robustness helps under moderate mismatch but has clear limits}

The mismatch experiments test whether the uncertainty-aware policy remains useful when the assumed state is not exactly correct. For residual CFO, a controller assumption of 500~Hz is compared with actual offsets between 1 and 5~kHz. On the evaluated 100-seed banks, B3 achieves approximately 87--88\% joint QoS, whereas B4 achieves 100\%. When a 20~m/s Doppler condition is assumed but the actual radial speed is 25--40~m/s, B3 reaches 86\% and B4 again reaches 100\%. Under interference under-modeling from assumed INR $-10$~dB to actual INR 0~dB, B3 falls to 18\%, while B4 reaches 86\%.

The benefit disappears when the mismatch leaves the region represented by the uncertainty model. At actual INR values of 10 or 20~dB, both policies fail in the tested bank. Likewise, when the controller assumes 12~dB SNR but the actual SNR is 8~dB, B4 joint QoS falls to 17\%; at 6~dB both policies fail. These cases are important because they show what the robust policy is not: it is not a replacement for accurate synchronization, interference suppression, or state estimation. Its protection is conditional on the uncertainty set being a reasonable description of the operating error.

\subsection{Receiver-level behavior explains the policy boundaries}

The waveform-level validation provides a physical explanation for the policy results. For differential communication at 8~dB, BER rises from approximately $9\times10^{-4}$ near zero residual frequency offset to approximately $3.2\times10^{-2}$ around 5~kHz. Residual Doppler or synchronization error at vehicular scales can therefore move an otherwise acceptable communication link across the BER threshold.

The sensing path shows a similarly sharp transition. For the short-range profile at 10~m and 3~m/s, range RMSE is several metres at very low IF-SNR and falls to centimetre scale once the estimator enters its usable regime. In the high-mobility validation case at approximately 20.28~m and 19.86~m/s, the target lies outside the short-range profile's unambiguous-velocity support but remains inside the high-mobility profile's support. This is exactly the type of state for which the physical gate must act before any probabilistic QoS comparison is meaningful.

\subsection{Reliability has both resource and computational costs}

The resource analysis is reported in terms of physical decision variables rather than interpreting the experiment-defined normalized cost as energy. The supplemental Pareto representation therefore exposes transmit-power fraction, chips per chirp, repetition factor, ADC samples per frame, effective rate, and sensing accuracy separately. One frozen B4 operating point, for example, uses 16 chips/chirp, repetition factor one, transmit-power fraction 0.2512, and 96,000 ADC samples/frame, with observed conditional joint QoS equal to one over 828 observations. The normalized optimization cost remains a dimensionless ordering index.

There is also a direct computational cost to confidence qualification. The finite-draw calibration compares B4 against a nested 4096-draw reference on 1,200 states. With only 32 draws, the 0.95 reliability target is mathematically unattainable under the one-sided Wilson rule even if every draw succeeds. At 64, 128, 256, and 512 draws, the observed feasibility-decision disagreement rates relative to the reference are 0.583\%, 0.333\%, 0.083\%, and 0\%, respectively. Exact action agreement converges more slowly, with selected-action disagreement of 3.583\%, 2.167\%, 1.250\%, and 0.833\%. The 4096-draw result is used only as a lower-Monte-Carlo-noise comparator, not as physical truth.

Runtime scales accordingly. Median B4 decision time on the reference Python/Linux host is approximately 32.17~ms at 64 draws, 63.81~ms at 128 draws, 127.19~ms at 256 draws, and 251.04~ms at 512 draws. At the frozen 512-draw setting, the 95th percentile is approximately 504.11~ms. These measurements rule out any claim that the present implementation already meets embedded real-time requirements. They instead motivate algorithmic work such as earlier action pruning, vectorized or parallel uncertainty evaluation, surrogate models, and sequential confidence tests.

\subsection{Interpretation}

Taken together, the evidence supports a narrower conclusion than a conventional robust-method-wins narrative. The physical gate identifies where the waveform family can represent the requested state at all. Within that physically admissible region, uncertainty-aware qualification reduces coverage but raises the reliability of the cases in which the controller acts. When uncertainty or model mismatch becomes too severe, the reliable region contracts further or disappears.

The independent supplemental bank reproduces the same qualitative pattern: B4 selects 12.50\% of 1,200 paired states with 100\% observed conditional joint QoS and a 98.23\% Wilson lower bound, whereas B3 selects 20.00\% with 80.83\% conditional joint QoS. The paired unconditional B4--B3 difference remains negative at $-0.03667$, with a 95\% interval of $[-0.04833,-0.02583]$. Distribution-shift banks also retain negative unconditional differences. The evidence therefore supports a reliability--availability--computation characterization of the PC-FMCW operating region, not universal policy dominance, global Pareto optimality, or deployment-level robustness.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper extends PC-FMCW ISAC from fixed-configuration functional feasibility toward physics-aware and reliability-constrained operation under high-mobility uncertainty. The proposed methodology separates whether a candidate waveform/profile is physically capable of supporting the requested range and radial velocity from whether the remaining admissible candidates satisfy joint communication and sensing reliability under uncertain channel and synchronization conditions. The resulting physical gate prevents impossible profiles from entering the statistical optimization, while the robust controller explicitly trades operating-region availability for higher conditional reliability.

The frozen paired benchmark does not show unconditional superiority of the robust controller. Instead, B4 selects only 12.23\% of evaluated cases, but succeeds in all 1,468 selected cases, with a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower bound of 99.816\%. The completed supplemental suite independently confirms the same qualitative trade-off: B4 selects 12.50\% of the 1,200-state paired full-metric bank with 100\% observed conditional joint QoS and a 98.23\% Wilson lower bound, whereas B3 selects 20.00\% with 80.83\% conditional joint QoS. At the same time, B4 is worse in unconditional joint QoS by $-0.03667$ with a 95\% paired-bootstrap interval of $[-0.04833,-0.02583]$, so universal or unconditional superiority is explicitly rejected.

Ablation and model-mismatch experiments further show that physical feasibility, uncertainty modeling, and the joint constraint are consequential. Moderate CFO, Doppler, and interference mismatch favor the robust controller on the tested banks, but severe SNR and interference mismatch remain failure modes. Finite-draw calibration against a 4096-draw reference gives a 0.083\% decision-disagreement rate at the frozen 256-draw setting, while 32 draws cannot attain the declared confidence target under the acceptance rule. These finite-bank results support the chosen simulation setting but are not universal guarantees.

The present implementation also exposes a computational limitation. At 256 uncertainty draws, B4 requires approximately 138.89~ms median decision time and 279.71~ms at the 95th percentile in the unoptimized Python/GitHub-Actions environment; at 512 draws the median rises to 272.81~ms. Hardware validation and algorithmic acceleration are therefore necessary before deployment-level latency, energy, or measurement claims can be made.

Overall, the central result is not that robust adaptation wins every operating point. Rather, physics-gated robust PC-FMCW adaptation makes the reliability boundary explicit: it identifies where joint sensing--communication service can be offered confidently, where the controller should abstain, and where the underlying waveform physics makes the requested service infeasible. The contribution is thus a confidence-qualified reliability--availability--complexity characterization of a finite PC-FMCW operating region, not a claim of global Pareto optimality or universal policy dominance.

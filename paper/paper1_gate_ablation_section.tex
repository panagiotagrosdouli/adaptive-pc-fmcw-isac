\section{Deterministic Physics-Gate Ablation}
To isolate the value of physical feasibility filtering from the later robust-policy study, we compare two deterministic nominal selectors over the same 54-action space. Both use the same nominal communication/sensing QoS screen and the same normalized-resource ranking. The only difference is that the gated selector first applies the canonical range and unambiguous-velocity support test, whereas the ungated selector ranks all actions without that test. No Monte-Carlo uncertainty draws, Wilson bounds, or B4 reliability qualification are used in this Paper 1 ablation.

The operating grid is deliberately boundary-aware rather than probabilistically weighted. It contains 14 range values from 0 to 60 m and 17 signed radial-velocity values from $-60$ to $60$ m/s, including samples immediately around the parking and high-mobility support boundaries. Of the resulting 238 states, 132 are physically representable by at least one declared profile and 106 are outside the support of the complete action set.

The primary comparison conditions on the 132 states for which the declared action space contains at least one physically valid configuration. In 114 of these 132 supportable states (86.36\%), the ungated selector chooses a physically invalid configuration despite the existence of a valid alternative in the same 54-action set. Equivalently, only 18 of the 132 supportable states (13.64\%) receive a physically valid ungated selection. The gated selector selects a physically valid action in all 132 supportable states and therefore makes zero physically invalid selections under the implemented support model. This conditional comparison isolates configuration-selection failure from states that no declared action can represent.

For completeness, over the full deliberately boundary-stressing grid, the ungated selector returns an action in all 238 states and 220 selections (92.44\%) violate the selected profile's deterministic range or velocity support. The remaining 106 states are outside the physical support of the complete action set, so their inclusion is useful for testing infeasibility detection but not for measuring recoverable selection error. Accordingly, 92.44\% is reported only as a stress-grid diagnostic and must not be interpreted as a prevalence estimate for real driving. Gating also distinguishes physical infeasibility of the declared action set from policy abstention rather than fabricating a nominally attractive but unsupported action.

The ablation therefore supports a narrow methodological claim: standard FMCW capability relations become operationally important when they are enforced as a hard first-stage constraint in finite-action configuration selection. The contribution is not a new FMCW range or Doppler equation; it is the explicit admissibility layer and the demonstrated failure mode that appears when that layer is omitted. The experiment does not show that all ungated ISAC controllers fail, nor does it establish field reliability. It shows that capability-blind ranking can select an unsupported waveform even when a physically valid alternative is already available, a failure that subsequent statistical averaging cannot repair after the unsupported action has been chosen.

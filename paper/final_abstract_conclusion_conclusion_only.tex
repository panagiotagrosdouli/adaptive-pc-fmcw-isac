\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper extends PC-FMCW ISAC from fixed-configuration functional feasibility toward physics-aware and reliability-constrained operation under high-mobility uncertainty. The proposed methodology separates whether a candidate waveform/profile is physically capable of supporting the requested range and radial velocity from whether the remaining admissible candidates satisfy joint communication and sensing reliability under uncertain channel and synchronization conditions. The resulting physical gate prevents impossible profiles from entering the statistical optimization, while the robust controller explicitly trades operating-region availability for higher conditional reliability.

The frozen paired benchmark does not show unconditional superiority of the robust controller. Instead, B4 selects only 12.23\% of evaluated cases, but succeeds in all 1,468 selected cases, with a one-sided 95\% Wilson lower bound of 99.816\%. The supplemental uncertainty sweep strengthens this interpretation: as uncertainty increases, B3 continues selecting a broader region while its conditional joint QoS degrades, whereas B4 progressively contracts its operating region and preserves much higher reliability until the most severe uncertainty setting, where the confidence-qualified 95\% target is no longer met.

Ablation and model-mismatch experiments further show that physical feasibility, uncertainty modeling, and the joint constraint are consequential. Moderate CFO, Doppler, and interference mismatch favor the robust controller, but severe SNR and interference mismatch remain failure modes. The present implementation also exposes a computational limitation: the 512-draw robust controller requires approximately 240~ms median decision time in the unoptimized Python/GitHub-Actions environment, compared with approximately 0.44~ms for deterministic joint adaptation.

Overall, the central result is not that robust adaptation wins every operating point. Rather, physics-gated robust PC-FMCW adaptation makes the reliability boundary explicit: it identifies where joint sensing--communication service can be offered confidently, where the controller should abstain, and where the underlying waveform physics makes the requested service infeasible. Hardware validation and algorithmic acceleration remain necessary before deployment-level claims can be made.
